Good Design

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

For Project 2, I used Adobe Illustrator and Canva as the primary technology tools for the logo design. I applied rhetorical principles in the Good Design project and considered ethics in Project 2 as well. I incorporated an AI-generated logo in my second design, which raised ethical concerns. Using AI for design wasn’t an ethical choice because AI bases its design decisions on internet references, potentially copying or misusing others' work.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

In Project 5, I applied design principles such as contrast, alignment, and repetition. I created contrast by using bright red against a white background with some black text. I achieved alignment by placing a large red circle in the center, which created a balanced look and symbolized Japan’s flag. I also used repetition by keeping the same font in the top left corner, rather than mixing fonts, to maintain a cleaner and more organized appearance

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

I workshopped all of the projects with my classmates except for Projects 1 and 2, which I completed after the deadline due to the add-drop period. I enjoyed workshopping because everyone had different perspectives, and it helped me realize where I might have missed the point on certain aspects. I definitely would’ve liked to workshop the first two projects for the same reason



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

I focused most on revision in Project 3, since it required me to revise my original logos based on user feedback and create a final design that reflected their recommendations. In the future, I hope not to shy away from making multiple revisions or hold back from adding to a design just because I might need to revise it later.

5. Learn to present your work.

I enjoy presenting my work in small workshops because the atmosphere feels more welcoming and less intimidating. However, I hope to become more confident presenting in front of the full class, since it’s a valuable skill and something that’s done frequently in UX design.
